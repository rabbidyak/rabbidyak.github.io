Insulin for a Match is an iOS matching game that helps you practice insulin brand names and their generic counterparts. Cards are dealt in two columns. You match a brand to a generic, and you can filter by fast, slow, or mixed action.

This page is the support and privacy page for the App Store listing.

## Support

Email [rabbidyak@gmail.com](mailto:rabbidyak@gmail.com) with the app name **Insulin for a Match** in the subject. Please include your device type and iOS version if you are reporting a problem.

## What the app does

- Match brand and generic insulin names in timed rounds
- Filter by acting speed: fast, slow, mixed, or all
- Optional labels that hide “brand” and “generic” so you have to recognize the names
- Product details drawn from the U.S. FDA [openFDA](https://open.fda.gov/) NDC Directory and drug labels
- A saved openFDA snapshot in the app; a live refresh from openFDA when the network is available

## Important disclaimer

Insulin for a Match is a tool for education. It is **not** medical advice and must not be used to treat or diagnose any issue. Names, NDCs, labels, and acting-speed tags come from public data sources and can be incomplete or out of date. Confirm any insulin product with a clinician and the official label.

## Privacy

The full legal notice is the [Privacy Policy](privacy/). A short summary of how this app uses data is below.

Insulin for a Match does not create an account with us, show ads, or sell your information.

| Data | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Settings (appearance, cards per round, brand/generic labels) | Stored on your device |
| Round score | Kept in memory for the current game on your device |
| Insulin catalog refresh | The app may download NDC listings and label text from openFDA over HTTPS. Those requests are not used to identify you |

We do not collect analytics, crash reports, or advertising IDs.

## Third-party sources

- [openFDA NDC Directory](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/ndc/) and [drug labels](https://open.fda.gov/apis/drug/label/) (U.S. FDA). openFDA is not a source of medical advice and is not for clinical use.
